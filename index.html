microbial_thermo

Thermodynamics for microbial physiology and biogeochemistry.

Given a redox couple or a whole reaction, it balances the chemistry, computes E°, E°′ and ΔG from a real thermodynamic database, and draws the figures a course needs — stacked half reactions, a redox tower, Eh–pH, Latimer and Frost–Ebsworth diagrams, and an interactive free-energy explorer.

Built as a teaching aid for a Colorado State course. Every result can be asked to show its work.

Interactive diagrams

Self-contained pages — no install, no kernel, nothing running behind them. Move the sliders and every curve recomputes in the browser.

Redox tower Every couple on one potential axis, with pH, temperature and the oxidised:reduced ratio on sliders. Watch couples cross. Arsenic redox diagrams Latimer, Frost–Ebsworth and Eh–pH together, sharing one decomposition. The pH slider is drawn on the Eh–pH panel, so the others are a slice through it. Free-energy explorer Hydrogenotrophic methanogenesis across its conditions — the metabolism that lives closest to the thermodynamic limit.

The syntrophic window, in three views

One partnership, one hydrogen axis. Neither organism can run alone: the ethanol oxidiser poisons itself with its own product, and the methanogen starves without it. Choose H2(g) from the dropdown on the first two, then see where the two curves overlap on the third.

The producer alone Ethanol oxidation by the S organism. As H₂ builds up it stops paying. The consumer alone Hydrogenotrophic methanogenesis. Below a few times 10⁻⁶ bar it is no longer exergonic at all. Both at once The lines cross, and the shaded band is the only range of H₂ where both partners pay. That band is the partnership.

Teaching notebooks

Thirteen notebooks, committed with their outputs and executed by the test suite, so a library change that breaks one is caught rather than discovered in class. They render directly on GitHub:

  • All notebooks — the full list
  • 1. Half reactions — couples, balancing, oxidation states, showing the work
  • 2. The redox tower — the tower, sweeps, and the syntrophic window
  • 3. Environmental affinity — speciation and what pays in a real porewater
  • Elements C, N, S, Fe, Mn, As, Se, Cr and U — one each, the same path through every one
  • 13. Methanogenesis — Type I and Type II, syntrophy, hydrogen floors, AOM

The ipywidgets cells in notebooks 02, 05, 07 and 10 need a live kernel, so GitHub shows those few cells blank. The pages above are the versions that run with nothing behind them.
